Blow again as the face returns

Lesson 3. The new breath does not sit in the lungs. The blow starts as the face meets the water.

Lesson 1 emptied the lungs while the face was down. Lesson 2 took one inhale with one goggle still in. The half that breaks the cycle is what you do with that new air. Holding it feels calm. It is the same hold as lesson 1, one breath later, and it ends the same way: a gasp, a lifted chin, hips down.

Total Immersion calls this “keep it moving”: begin exhaling as soon as you finish inhaling, and do not pause the exchange. U.S. Masters Swimming says the same return: after the inhale, the head goes back and the exhale starts, unhurried, while the mouth is under. This lesson is that instant, still at the wall. Kicking and pulling wait.

The move

  • Chest-deep water. Both hands on the wall.
  • Face in, blow, roll until one goggle stays, one inhale. Same as the last two lessons.
  • Roll the face back. Bubbles start as the mouth meets the water. No quiet moment with full lungs.
  • Keep blowing until the lungs are nearly empty, then roll again.

Eight cycles. If the face is back and the water is quiet, that one does not count. Put the face in and start the blow sooner.

Before you start: at the moment the mouth goes under, are you holding the new breath, or already blowing it out?
