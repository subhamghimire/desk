One goggle stays in the water

Lesson 2. The inhale is a roll, not a lift. One goggle stays under.

Lesson 1 got the air out while your face was down. The inhale is the next half of the same breath. You do not look up to find it. You roll until the mouth clears, and you stop while one goggle is still in the water. If both goggles come out, the chin came up, and the hips drop.

Total Immersion teaches the breath as one goggle above the surface, head still in line with the spine. U.S. Masters Swimming says the same thing from the other end: lifting the head sinks the hips. This lesson is that cue, standing still. Kicking and pulling wait.

The move

  • Chest-deep water. Both hands on the wall, or holding the gutter.
  • Face in. Blow out the whole time, the same blow as lesson 1.
  • When the lungs are nearly empty, roll your shoulder toward the ceiling until the mouth clears. One goggle stays in the water. One inhale.
  • Roll the face back in before that breath is spent, and blow again.

Eight of those. If both goggles come out, that one does not count. Put the face back and roll less.

Before you start: which goggle is still in the water at the moment you inhale?
