Blow the air out before you need more

Lesson 1. The gasp is what stands you up. The exhale is what lets you stay in.

A beginner holds a full breath, puts their face in, and waits until they need air. By then the only option is to lift the head and gulp. Lifting the head drops the hips, the legs sink, and the next breath is harder than the last. The panic is the breath being held, not the water.

The air has to leave while the face is still in. A steady blow through the nose or the mouth, the whole time the face is down. When the lungs are nearly empty, turn just far enough for one inhale, then put the face back and start blowing again. You never have to steal a breath in a hurry, because the old air is already gone.

The move

  • Stand in water at chest depth. Hold the wall if you want it.
  • Face in. Blow out the whole time, a long "hmm" through the nose or a steady stream of bubbles.
  • Turn the head to the side until the mouth clears. One inhale. Not a lift of the chin.
  • Face back in before the inhale is spent, and blow again.

Ten of those, unhurried, is the lesson. If the hips drop, the chin came up. Put the face back down and the hips follow.
